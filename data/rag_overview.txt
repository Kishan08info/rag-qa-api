Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an architecture that combines a retriever with a
generator. Instead of relying solely on a language model's parametric knowledge, the system
first retrieves relevant passages from an external knowledge base and then conditions the
model's answer on those passages. This grounds responses in real documents and reduces
hallucination.

A typical RAG pipeline has three stages. First, ingestion: documents are split into chunks,
each chunk is embedded into a dense vector, and the vectors are stored in a vector index
such as FAISS. Second, retrieval: the user's question is embedded with the same model and
the top-k most similar chunks are fetched via approximate nearest neighbor search. Third,
generation: the retrieved chunks are placed into the language model's prompt as context,
and the model produces an answer citing its sources.

Key design decisions include chunk size and overlap (too large dilutes relevance, too small
loses context), the choice of embedding model, and whether to re-rank retrieved passages
before generation. Evaluation usually measures retrieval recall (did we fetch the right
chunks?) and answer faithfulness (is every claim supported by the context?).
