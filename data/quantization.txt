Model quantization is the process of reducing the numerical precision of a neural network's
weights and activations, for example converting 32-bit floating point (FP32) values to 8-bit
integers (INT8). The goal is to shrink the model's memory footprint and speed up inference,
which is critical for on-device AI where smartphones, IoT devices, and embedded NPUs have
strict power and memory budgets.

Common approaches include post-training quantization (PTQ), which converts a trained model
without retraining, and quantization-aware training (QAT), which simulates low precision
during training to preserve accuracy. PTQ is faster to apply but can lose 1-3% accuracy on
sensitive models, while QAT typically recovers most of that loss at the cost of a retraining run.

In practice, INT8 quantization can reduce model size by 4x and cut inference latency
significantly on hardware with INT8 acceleration, such as mobile NPUs and edge TPUs.
For large language models, 4-bit weight quantization (e.g. GPTQ, AWQ) is now standard for
fitting 7B+ parameter models onto consumer GPUs and high-end phones.
